1 Abstract

2

Long-span arch bridges are widely adopted in mountainous areas of western China,

3 yet the rapid construction requirements of main arch segments under complex working

4 conditions are barely satisfied by conventional manual welding. To address this

5 technical gap, the dedicated ring-welding robot for main arch segments and its

6 collaborative obstacle-avoidance welding path planning method are proposed. Point

7 cloud, kinematic and planning offset models of the robot and target arch segments are

8 established, and multi-objective collaborative path planning under multi-obstacle

9 scenarios is realized by integrating preset ring-welding paths and rib plate obstacle-

10 avoidance paths. Numerical simulations and experimental validation results show that

11 the proposed method satisfies all collision safety and welding posture requirements,

12 and guarantees smooth motion in both joint and task spaces, with a 35 mm minimum

13 safe clearance and torch inclination controlled between -7.156° and 28.075°. It also

14 achieves the sub-millimeter Grade E end-effector positioning accuracy and uniform

15 defect-free coating formation.

The core challenge in developing the ring-welding robot for arch ribs is to plan

5 necessary to construct the environmental model of the main arch segment and the

6 kinematic model of the robot. Among them, the environmental modelling for welding

9 the geometric-algebra, or the data-driven formulation [17-18]. However, the weld seam

10 of long-span arch demands higher geometric fidelity than the rib itself, and kinematic

11 maps that treat the joint angle as the sole variable cannot compensate for the

12 continuously varying curvature and diameter. Therefore, the existing single-precision

13 and variable-planning models cannot be directly applied to the welding of variable-

14 curved surface segments in long-span arch bridge.

Finally, for the obstacle-avoidance target, it is necessary to consider obstacle-

11 avoidance paths that account for multiple obstacles based on the annular welding path.

12 The mainstream algorithms for obstacle-avoidance path planning are the sampling

13 method and the potential field method. Hippolitus et al. [22,23-24] improved the sampling

14 method of RRT by using backpropagation and bidirectional search, and thus achieve

15 rapid path planning for robot obstacle avoidance. Zucker et al. [25] constructed the

16 objective function by combining the potential field cost and the smoothness cost, and

17 they then quantify and optimize the path, achieving a smoother obstacle-avoidance path

18 for the robot. The path planning method that focuses on the single obstacle-avoidance

19 target cannot be applied directly to the welding tasks encountered in the multi-obstacle

20 scenarios of long-span arch bridge.

9 2 Ring-welding and planning process for main arch segments

10

During the construction process, the main arch segments of long-span arch bridge

11 are usually lifted separately and then connected together [26]. The overall construction

12 process mainly includes the segment pre-assembly, hoisting and alignment, and

13 circumferential welding and closure, as shown in Figure 2(a). Step 1: The processed

14 main arch segments are transported from the factory to the construction site for pre-

15 assembly. The structural dimensions and processing quality of the main arch segments

16 are checked. Step 2: As per the construction drawings, lift and align the main arch rib

17 segment, then complete the initial assembly by bolting the matching connection gusset

18 plates. Step 3: Ring-welding between each main arch segment is performed sequentially.

19 The assembly and closure of the main arch segments of the long-span arch bridge are

20 completed. Moreover, the manual welding is replaced by the self-developed ring-

21 welding robot, with complete the ring-welding between the main arch segments. The

17 the obstacle-avoidance welding of the ring-welding robot is realised by leveraging the
