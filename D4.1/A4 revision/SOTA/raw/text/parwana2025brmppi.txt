BR-MPPI: Barrier-Rate Guided MPPI for Enforcing Multiple Inequality Constraints with Learned Signed Distance Fields
Hardik Parwana1,∗, Taekyung Kim1,∗, Kehan Long2, Bardh Hoxha3, Hideki Okamoto3, Georgios Fainekos3, and Dimitra Panagou1

arXiv:2506.07325v2 [cs.RO] 28 Sep 2026

Abstract— Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control provides a sampling-based framework for optimal control, while Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) provide a principled means of enforcing safety constraints. We introduce BR-MPPI, which integrates CBF-like conditions into MPPI’s control sampling procedure. CBFs impose inequality constraints that bound the rate of change of barrier functions using a class-K function of the barrier value. We instead impose the CBF condition as an equality constraint using a parametric linear class-K function and augment the system state with its parameter. The parameter’s time derivative serves as an additional control input optimized by MPPI. We further design a cost function that promotes parameter values consistent with Nagumo’s condition at the safe-set boundary, thereby encouraging safety. The resulting multiple state- and control-dependent equality constraints pose a challenge for random control sampling. We address this through state transformations and control projections inspired by manifold path planning that map sampled controls onto the constraint manifold. We also incorporate learned signed distance fields to represent robot geometry and reduce computation time. Simulations demonstrate improved sample efficiency over vanilla MPPI and higher navigation success rates across five robot models compared with safety-oriented MPPI variants. Hardware experiments on a quadrotor further demonstrate the method’s ability to navigate constrained environments near safe-set boundaries. [Project Page]1 [Code] [Video]
I. INTRODUCTION
Autonomous systems increasingly incorporate learning-
based techniques into real-time operation, from active learn-
ing for estimating unknown quantities to offline-learned
dynamics models and signed distance fields. Deploying these
systems in constrained environments requires real-time kin-
odynamic planning that accounts for robot geometry and
permits operation near safe-set boundaries. Model Predic-
tive Path Integral (MPPI) control [1] and Control Barrier
Function (CBF)-based quadratic programs (QPs) [2] offer
complementary approaches to this problem. MPPI optimizes
trajectories over a finite horizon but does not inherently
guarantee inequality constraint satisfaction, whereas standard

CBF-QP controllers enforce instantaneous safety conditions but can be myopic and yield suboptimal trajectories.
Vanilla MPPI [1] solves unconstrained optimal control problems by sampling control sequences, evaluating the resulting state trajectories, and updating the nominal sequence through a cost-weighted average of the sampled controls. Cost penalties provide a soft treatment of inequality constraints, leaving MPPI susceptible to violations, particularly with few samples [3]. Penalty weights may also require extensive tuning to navigate close to constraint boundaries without violating them. Among the extensions developed to improve MPPI [4]–[7], we focus on CBF-based approaches to constraint satisfaction.
A straightforward approach is to filter MPPI commands through a CBF-QP [3]. Alternatively, the same work constructs trust regions by optimizing the mean and covariance of the control sampling distribution subject to CBF conditions. This requires a semidefinite program (SDP) at each time step of every sampled trajectory, with fewer samples used to mitigate the computational burden. Shield-MPPI [8] for deterministic systems and the method in [9] for stochastic systems penalize CBF inequality violations and subsequently apply gradient-based nonlinear optimization to enforce the constraints, subject to convergence. GS-MPPI [10] instead applies a closed-form CBF-based safety correction.
SC-MPPI [11] incorporates barrier-state-based safe feedback into sampling. The related formulation in [12] augments the robot state with barrier values that diverge at constraint boundaries. These added states are functions of the robot state: their evolution is determined by the robot trajectory,

rather than governed by additional control inputs. We introduce Barrier-Rate Guided MPPI (BR-MPPI),
which incorporates CBF-like conditions directly into sampling without the inequality-constrained optimization used in [3]. CBFs impose inequality constraints that bound the rate of change of barrier functions using a class-K function of the barrier value. For each constraint, we instead impose the CBF condition as an equality constraint using a parametric linear class-K function and augment the system state with its parameter rather than the barrier value. The parameter’s time derivative serves as an additional control input optimized by MPPI, allowing online adaptation of barrier evolution without requiring singular barriers. We further design a cost function that promotes parameter values consistent with Nagumo’s condition at the safe-set boundary, thereby encouraging safety.
The resulting multiple state- and control-dependent equality constraints pose a challenge for random sampling, since sampled controls need not satisfy them simultaneously. Inspired by manifold path planning, we introduce state transformations and state-dependent control projections that map sampled controls onto the constraint manifold. For controlaffine dynamics, a first-order approximation yields an analytical projection. This formulation can induce multimodal trajectory distributions in the robot’s original state space, with sampled paths passing on different sides of obstacles, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to consider: 1) MPPI with multiple state- and controldependent equality constraints; 2) set invariance theory to promote constraint satisfaction in an augmented state space accounting for barrier values and their rates of change; and 3) MPPI-based design or adaptation of CBF class-K function parameters. Although parameter adaptation has been studied for CBF-QPs [13]–[17], it has not been studied for MPPI.
Simulation case studies demonstrate improved sample efficiency over vanilla MPPI. In 100-trial benchmarks for each of five robot models, BR-MPPI with analytical barriers achieves higher success rates than four safety-oriented MPPI variants, with 87–100% success and zero unsafe trials. We also incorporate signed distance fields learned from robot geometry, reducing mean command computation time by factors of 2.10–2.52 relative to analytical barriers, with model-dependent effects on navigation outcomes. Quadrotor hardware experiments further demonstrate navigation in constrained environments near safe-set boundaries.

C. Control Barrier Functions
CBFs are a common tool for enforcing safety constraints in a controller. For a linear class-K function νi(r) = αir with 0 < αi ≤ 1, the following condition on the applied input preserves hi(xt) ≥ 0:

hi(xt + F (xt, uxt )) − hi(xt) ≥ −αihi(xt). (4)

For multiple constraints, the same input must satisfy all such inequalities simultaneously. In practice, (4) is often imposed using a constraint function hi as a candidate CBF, even when its feasibility over the entire safe set is not guaranteed. Intuitively, the CBF condition upper bounds the rate at which the xt can approach the safe set boundary: it allows h(xt+1) < h(xt) when h(xt) > 0 but requires h(xt+1) ≥ h(xt) when h(xt) = 0. The rate is governed by α. Since finding valid CBFs with desired performance characteristics (e.g., aggressive or conservative) is difficult α is usually tuned offline or online.

Remark 3. Sampling inputs subject to equality constraints is actively studied for path planning on manifolds [24], e.g., for closed-chain systems with state constraints. Our projection is inspired by such works, as each equality constraint in (12) induces a manifold for the state-action pair. Consequently, our method inherits discretization errors associated with manifold constraints. While (17) performs well in practice, its first-order approximation can introduce errors for highly nonlinear h, potentially causing instability. One could recompute the tangent space of hi when this error exceeds a threshold, as in [25] for kinodynamic RRT; we leave its application to real-time control for future work. Note that the dynamics (1) itself defines a manifold inherently followed by MPPI trajectories. However, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first work considering multiple state- and inputdependent equality constraints in MPPI.

E. Discussion
While applying MPPI to the augmented system (13) can be viewed as a state transformation, it has several important

consequences. Here, we discuss its differences and improvements over standard MPPI and CBF approaches.
1) Improvement of MPPI Through CBF: First, we use the well-established theory of CBFs and set invariance to guide inequality constraint satisfaction. Our algorithm handles inequality constraints through two steps: 1) replacing each inequality constraint with a set of equality constraints parameterized by the additional state α˜, and 2) using manifold planning techniques to satisfy these equality constraints. Their satisfaction is promoted through Nagumo’s theorem near the constraint boundary.
Second, Fig. 1 shows sampled trajectories passing on different sides of obstacles. The state-dependent projection transforms Gaussian pseudo-input samples before rollout, changing the resulting trajectory distribution. Our method can still sample trajectories inside obstacles, but avoids such solutions more effectively in the reported examples.
2) Improvement of CBF Through MPPI: Finding valid CBFs remains an active research area, and candidate CBFs are commonly used in CBF-QP controllers to impose (4). A candidate CBF may yield infeasible CBF-QPs for some states, while even a valid CBF may be overly conservative for a given class-K function. Several works therefore adapt the class-K function offline or online. Our algorithm can be viewed as adapting the class-K function online to improve performance while promoting safety. Moreover, although we use CBF-like conditions, constraint satisfaction is enforced through Nagumo’s theorem only near the boundary, while α˜ can vary in R in the safe-set interior. Our method is therefore less restrictive than a fixed-class-K CBF. Further analysis of the relationship between our MPPI solutions and valid CBFs is left for future work.

BR-MPPI reaches the goal without collision in all five representative trials. In particular, panels (c)–(e) illustrate cases where the competing methods collide or fail to reach the goal.
In the 100-trial benchmarks shown in Fig. 4, BR-MPPI achieves the highest success rate for every robot model (87– 100%) and is the only method with zero unsafe trials across all five models. The remaining BR-MPPI trials terminate in timeout or deadlock. Its mean command computation time ranges from 26.39 to 145.27 ms, exceeding those of MPPICBF and GS-MPPI but remaining below that of SC-MPPI for every model.
These violations do not contradict the baselines’ conditional safety guarantees. Distance-based candidate CBFs may be infeasible under bounded inputs; SC-MPPI relies on barrier-state feedback that is locally valid around the nominal trajectory; and GS-MPPI can lose its CBF guarantee when actuator saturation modifies the closed-form safe control.

C. Comparative Study: Exact vs Learned SDF
We compare analytic and learned signed-distance barriers within BR-MPPI over 100 trials for each of the three robot models in Table I. We retain the tuned BR-MPPI controller parameters from Section IV-B and change only the barrierfunction implementation from the analytical distance representation to its learned counterpart. The results are shown in Table I. The optimized neural SDF reduces mean command latency by factors of 2.10–2.52, with model-dependent effects on navigation outcomes. For the dynamic unicycle, both formulations achieve 100 successful trials without collisions. For the planar quadrotor, the neural formulation remains collision-free, while success decreases from 98% to 94%. For the unicycle, success decreases from 87% to 84%, and 11 trials terminate in collision. While safety depends on the accuracy of the learned geometry, these results demonstrate the computational benefit of learned barriers in achieving real-time execution speeds.

VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
We presented BR-MPPI, which guides control sampling through CBF-like equality constraints by augmenting the state with barrier-rate parameters and projecting sampled controls. Simulations demonstrate improved sample efficiency over vanilla MPPI and higher success rates than safety-oriented MPPI variants, while quadrotor experiments demonstrate navigation near safe-set boundaries. Learned signed distance fields reduce computation time, with modeldependent effects on navigation outcomes. Future work includes incorporating higher-order CBFs, establishing theoretical guarantees for constraint satisfaction under actuation constraints, and extending hardware evaluations to a broader range of robotic systems.
