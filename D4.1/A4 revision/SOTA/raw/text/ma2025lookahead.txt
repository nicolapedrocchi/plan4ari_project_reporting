Abstract—To address the inefficiency and unstable posture synchronization caused by frequent start-stop motions in smallsegment machining, we propose a real-time look-ahead trajectory planning algorithm that combines logarithmic-space virtual line fitting with a seven-segment quintic polynomial. The integration of logarithmic-space mapping with Bezier curves enables positionposture decoupling, effectively eliminating the posture oscillation inherent in traditional coupling approaches. A seven-segment quintic polynomial combined with a bidirectional scanning module dynamically adjusts the trajectory to satisfy real-time path constraints. Simulation results demonstrate the algorithm's feasibility and real-time performance.
Keywords—small segment, posture synchronization, Bezier curve, Look-ahead
I. INTRODUCTION
With the advancement of industrial automation, robots are increasingly employed to machine complex curved surfaces[1]. For six-axis robotic systems, enhancing motion performance is critical to achieving high machining accuracy.
Path continuity assurance methods are primarily categorized into joint-space smoothing and end-effector-space smoothing approaches. Joint-space smoothing presents mathematical control challenges due to introduced nonlinear errors. End-effector-space smoothing comprises: global smoothing using parametric curve approximation, and angle smoothing employing transition angle insertion. Global curve fitting methods exhibit high computational complexity as they require exact interpolation through all specified points. These methods lack real-time capability and offer limited curve

The complete path is discretized into segments with transitions implemented using linear, circular arc, or spline interpolations. These transition techniques have found widespread adoption in path smoothing applications. Sun[3] developed a real-time lookahead interpolation algorithm employing cubic B-splines for dynamic local transitions between path segments. Wang et al. Huang[5] designed a seven-control-point B-spline transition curve to guarantee smooth path continuity. Liu [6]implemented a lead-point-fitted B-spline compression algorithm for continuous micro-segment paths, enhanced by quintic B-splines to improve inter-segment continuity.
The second major challenge in 6D path smoothing involves posture synchronization, as robotic and machine tool path points incorporate both position and orientation data Numerous-path smoothing studies have addressed posture synchronization requirements. Yang[7] developed an analytical method for 6R robots that mathematically guarantees synchronization between positional and orientational trajectories. Wan[8] utilized the arc-length analytical properties of asymmetric PH splines to synchronize positional and directional variations. Li et al. [9] employed dual NURBS curves for linear path smoothing and optimization, with experimental validation of the method's superiority. Following G2-continuous path construction through spline-integrated discrete micro-segments, trajectory planning becomes essential to maintain acceleration and jerk within robotic limits[10]. Common trajectory planning acceleration/deceleration algorithms comprise polynomial, trapezoidal, and S-curve profiles[11][12]. The global preflight module generates a dynamically

feasible velocity profile through bidirectional scanning that

propagates and refines speed constraints. The process begins

with a forward scan, initialized with a zero starting velocity. At

each segment between waypoints 𝑖 and 𝑖 + 1 , the module

checks if the desired speed violates the system's kinematic

limits. A violation triggers an update of the velocity constraint

at waypoint 𝑖 + 1 to the maximum achievable value, which is

then backward-propagated to preceding points to ensure

consistent deceleration. However, this forward pass typically

results in a non-zero final velocity. To enforce a safe stop, a

reverse scan is subsequently executed, initializing the endpoint

with a zero-velocity constraint and repeating the same

adjustment logic in reverse. This bidirectional process

iteratively tightens the velocity profile, guaranteeing a

trajectory that is globally feasible and adheres to the boundary

conditions of zero initial and final velocity.

V. CONCLUSION
The aforementioned path-smoothing algorithm, which incorporates a look-ahead function, was validated on a threeaxis motion platform. A three-dimensional path comprising a five-pointed star and a semicircle was generated. Data simulations were then conducted to plot the corresponding velocity, acceleration, and jerk profiles. Subsequently, the quaternion-based pose synchronization method for the virtual attitude trajectory was independently validated and compared against a method utilizing spherical linear interpolation. The irregular composite path, consisting of a five-pointed star and a semicircle, was discretized into 75 linear segments with an average length of 1 mm. The experimental setup and resultant path are illustrated in Figure 4. The velocity, acceleration, and jerk limits were set to 2.0×10³ mm/s, 4.0×10⁴ mm/s², and 1.8×10⁷ mm/s³, respectively. As shown in Figure 5, the lookahead algorithm prevented the velocity from dropping to zero at the start of each segment. Velocity decreased significantly at only three points along the entire path, thereby enhancing time efficiency and avoiding excessive jerk. Furthermore, both the acceleration and jerk profiles remain continuous throughout the motion.

the traditional SLERP method in key performance metrics. In

terms of motion accuracy, the proposed method reduces the

maximum angular error and RMS angular error by

approximately 55% and 70%, respectively. For motion

smoothness, the proposed method improves the smoothness

index by approximately 52% and reduces the peak angular

acceleration by over 90% compared to SLERP. Consequently,

the proposed method effectively mitigates severe motion jitter

and yields more stable and smooth trajectories. Collectively,

these results demonstrate the method's capability to achieve

high accuracy and smoothness simultaneously.

In summary, compared to previous methods, the algorithm

proposed in this paper offers the following advantages:
(1) G2 continuity is achieved, and both line segments and curves are incorporated.
(2) Quaternions are used to represent posture, thus avoiding the issue of Euler angle singularity.
(3) The virtual posture curve fitting method enables the independent design of position and posture curves.
(4) The seven-segment quintic polynomial acceleration and deceleration trajectory planning enhances the flexibility of motion planning.
