An analytical tool path smoothing algorithm for robotic machining with the consideration of redundant kinematics
Jixiang Yang *, Qi Qi , Abulikemu Adili , Han Ding
State Key Laboratory of Intelligent Manufacturing Equipment and Technology, School of Mechanical Science and Engineering, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Wuhan 430074, China

ARTICLE INFO
Keywords: Robotic machining Tool path Kinematics Redundancy Spline

ABSTRACT
In the machining of complex parts with free-formed surfaces, robots are widely employed due to their advantages of a large operating space and high flexibility. The industrial robot with 6 degrees-of-freedom (DOF) has an extra redundant degree of freedom around the tool axis, which does not affect the tool pose related to the workpieces but influences the robot’s joint configuration. The motion performance and machining efficiency can be improved by optimizing the redundant angle. Based on this, an analytical path smoothing algorithm for 6-DOF robots with the consideration of redundant kinematics is proposed to improve the robot’s dynamic performance. The tool tip position and orientation are fit with the analytical 5th degree Pythagorean-Hodograph (PH) spline to satisfy C2 continuity, respectively. Therefore, the 5th degree polynomial spline with minimum acceleration is adopted to fit the redundant rotation angle and the tool tip position arc length. Then the tool path position spline, tool orientation spline, and redundant rotary angle spline are all synchronized to the tool position displacement, which makes it convenient to do interpolation along the tool path. The minimum time feed planning method considering the joint dynamic constraints is adopted to interpolate motion commands. Experimental results show that the motion efficiency of the robot in the same test path increases by 33.97 % compared with the regular spline without considering acceleration of the redundant angle spline. Meanwhile, the proposed tool path smoothing method effectively mitigates the joint vibration with a maximum reduction of 65.05 %, without sacrificing motion accuracy.

1. Introduction
Traditional industrial robots with six degrees of freedom (DOF) are commonly used to transport workpieces from one position to another. The trajectory plan for such carrying motions usually requires precise control over velocity and acceleration, while maintaining precise contour accuracy during the motion process is less important. However, as industrial robots have improved in terms of positioning accuracy and structural rigidity, they are increasingly utilized in manufacturing industries including milling [1–4], polishing [5–9], and deburring [10, 11], which fully show their larger workspace, lower cost, and higher flexibility [12,13]. When it comes to 5-axis machining tasks, addressing the dynamics and motion precision throughout the entire tool path profile becomes critical [14,15]. The generation and optimization of the contour motion path in robotic machining present a significantly higher degree of complexity compared with the "point-to-point" type motions. In recent years, many researchers have made significant progress in this

field. Generally, commercial software typically generates robot motion
commands that contain discrete positions and orientations. However, tangential discontinuity in these discrete linear tool paths can result in fluctuating velocity and acceleration. This leads to significant vibrations in the robot’s motion. Therefore, to ensure steady robotic machining motions, the tool path must be smoothed before being executed by the motor drives. Tool path smoothing methods can be categorized as either joint space methods or workpiece frame methods, depending on the operation space. In the joint space method, the robot motion commands of individual joints are smoothed directly [16–18]. This method allows for the direct execution of the smoothed tool path results by joint drives, eliminating the need for additional inverse kinematics computations. However, this method can make it challenging to restrain the deviation errors of the tool path profile when smoothing tool path commands of the joint due to the nonlinear kinematics. However, the workpiece frame method, involves direct smoothing of the tool path commands in the

workpiece coordinate system (WCS) under the constraints of geometric deviation errors [19,20], and then the commands are transformed to joint displacements before being sent to joint drives [21–23]. This study focuses on the smoothing method of the 6-DOF robot in the WCS.
Unlike 3-axis machining, where smoothing is required only for the tool position commands, 5-axis or 6-axis machining tasks necessitate the smoothing of the tool orientation direction as well [24–26]. Based on the synchronization method of the tool path position and orientation spline, smoothing methods can be classified into two categories: coupled methods and decoupled methods. In the coupled method, the fitting of tool path position and orientation splines is accomplished using the same parameter. For example, Langeron et al. [27] developed dual B-splines to represent the tool pose, one spline was used to fit the tool path position, while another one was used to fit points equidistant to the tip point along the orientation vector. Wan et al. [28] proposed a corner smoothing algorithm to smooth the tool tip position and tool orientation by constructing asymmetrical PH splines, which resulted in higher machining efficiency. To ensure that the two splines have equal distances after fitting, Huang et al. [29] proposed a novel curve “airthoid” to smooth the corners of the tool position in the WCS. Bi et al. [30] introduced a B-spline in the quaternion space rather than the Cartesian space. However, the orientation may exhibit large fluctuations at short linear segments for complex surface parts. The coupled method cannot accurately reflect the simultaneous variance tendency, subsequently affecting the dynamic performance of the orientation, which has been proven by the study of Yuen et al. [31]. To address this issue, algorithms were developed to smooth the position and orientation individually, fully reflecting the fluctuation of the tool path position and orientation. Then the position and orientation spline parameters were mapped to the tool tip displacement to synchronize them along the tool path [32,33]. Li et al. [34] proposed the use of triple NURBS curves to fit the position and axis of the tool.
Unlike 5-axis machine tools, which are affected by only two rotary axes for tool-axis orientation, 6-DOF robots with their six rotary joints require more intricate processing. This is because the tool orientation may be affected by all six rotary joints, depending on the robot’s kinematics. Yang et al. [35] introduced an analytical continuous tool path corner smoothing algorithm specifically designed for 6R robot manipulators. This algorithm involves the construction of micro B-splines to represent the tool path position in the Cartesian system, while the tool orientation is represented by B-splines in the Euler angle frame. To ensure synchronization between the tool-axis direction and tool tip position, the remaining linear segments are replaced with specially constructed B-splines. Using this approach, the algorithm achieves smooth tool path corners by coordinating the tool tip displacement with the tool axis direction and position. However, there are two issues need to be further solved in the study of tool path smoothing for robotic machining. First, compared with 5-axis CNC machining, robot machining has one redundant rotary freedom. Although the redundant rotary freedom does not change the tool pose in the WCS, it affects the robot’s joint motions via inverse kinematics. Therefore, the smoothing of the redundant rotary angle directly affects the dynamic performance of the joint commands. While the discrete solution of the redundant rotary freedom has been studied [36,37], there are not tool path smoothing algorithms that consider the redundant rotation of 6R robot machining. Second, existing algorithms of 6R robot machining are mainly based on NURBS and B-splines, where iterative calculations are needed to estimate the tool length because the spline arc length lacks an explicit function with the spline parameter that restricts the calculation efficiency. Moreover, the estimation errors of tool lengths induce fluctuations of feed rate after tool path interpolation. Although some analytical tool path smoothing algorithms are reported to three-axis [38, 39] and five-axis machine tools [38] by constructing special splines that have analytical solutions of the arc-length, they cannot be directly adopted to 6-DOF robotic machining owing to the more complex and redundant kinematics of 6-DOF robot. Therefore, the development of

analytical robot path smoothing algorithms for robotic 5-axis machining task is crucial, especially when considering the presence of the redundant DOF in robotic machining. These algorithms play a significant role in achieving precise and efficient motion planning, ensuring smooth tool path execution, and optimizing overall machining performance in complex 5-axis machining tasks.
This paper proposes an analytical C2 continuous path smoothing algorithm for robotic machining with the consideration of redundant kinematics. This paper makes several significant contributions to the field, which includes the following:
(1) The proposed algorithm considers the redundant kinematics of a 6-DOF robot in order to optimize tool path smoothing for robotic 5-axis machining task.
(2) The algorithm analytically calculates the tool path position displacements and control points of all curves, making it suitable for high efficiency calculations during robot machining processes.
(3) The algorithm employs a 5th-degree polynomial spline with minimum acceleration to fit the redundant orientation angle to the arc length, ensuring lower vibrations in robotic machining.
(4) The C2 continuity of the tool path is achieved by optimizing the federate under the constraints of the joint velocity, acceleration and jerk, which results in acceleration continuity of the interpolated joint commands.

3. Tool path generation for 6-DOF robot machining
3.1. Overall introduction of the tool path smoothing
Due to the presence of the redundant rotary angle in the robotic machining tasks, this paper proposes a global analytical path smoothing method considering the redundant angle to fully harness the dynamic performance of the robot. The tool tip position is smoothed in Cartesian space by using a 5th degree Pythagorean-Hodograph (PH) spline P(u) that is capable of online high-speed analytical calculation. To construct the tool orientation spline, the discrete orientation vector O = [Ox, Oy, Oz]T is firstly transformed into the Euler angle space Θ = [α, β]T. Subsequently, the tool orientation is smoothed by creating a 5th degree plane PH spline Θ(ω) in the Euler angle space. The redundant rotation angles are smoothed by constructing a 5th degree polynomial spline γ(s) of the rotary angle γ related to the tool tip arc length s. Since both the position spline parameter u and redundant angle spline can establish analytical relationships with the tool tip arc length s, so only the tool orientation parameter ω is need to synchronize with s along the tool path. Therefore, the tool tip position, orientation and redundant rotation commands are smoothed and mapped to the tool tip displacements with functions P(u(s)), Θ(ω(s)) and γ(s) respectively. By doing feed rate plan along the tool tip displacements, the time-stamped arc-length s(t) is calculated. Finally, the time-stamped motion commands θ(t) of robot joints are obtained through robot kinematics based on the planed position commands P(u(s(t))), orientation commands Θ(ω(s(t))) and redundant angle commands γ(s(t)) are obtained. The flowchart of the proposed method is presented in Fig. 4.

4. Experiments
The proposed algorithm is implemented in MATLAB, version 2020, on a Windows 11 system. The experiment is conducted on a UR10 robot with a 3 kg load as shown in Fig. 9. The interpolation period in the feed planning is precisely set to be 0.008 s. The constraints of the robot position are set as vmax = 25mm/s, amax = 2000mm/s2, jmax = 10000mm/s3. The joint constraints are listed in Table 1.
The test path is shown in Fig. 10, while Fig. 11 presents the global smoothed tool position spline, orientation spline, and redundant angle spline. To demonstrate the high efficiency calculation capability of the global PH spline, the total time required for the spline construction and the arc length calculation is compared with that of the B-spline method. It takes 74.8 ms for the proposed algorithm to finish the calculation, which is significantly faster than the B-spline method that requires 240.1 ms. The comparative results indicate that the proposed method can achieve highly accurate online calculations with improved efficiency.
To demonstrate the benefits of the proposed redundant angle smoothing algorithm with the consideration of acceleration constraints, C2 continuous spline for (s, γ) without the consideration of the acceleration constraints of the redundant angle is compared here.
The same feed planning method is applied to interpolate the joint motion commands of the robot while adhering to the corresponding constraints of tangential velocity, acceleration, and jerk. The result of the feed planning is presented in Fig. 12, which shows that the average velocity achieved by the proposed algorithm is 14.337 mm/s. This is faster than the regular spline method without the consideration of the acceleration constraints of the redundant angle that achieves an average velocity of 9.467 mm/s. As a consequence of this increase in velocity, the motion cycle of the proposed method is reduced by 33.97%, from 26.728 s to 17.648 s, when compared with the regular C2 continuous spline method. These results indicate that the proposed method effectively guarantees the motion dynamic constraints of the robot and enhances motion efficiency.

5. Conclusion
This article presents an analytical C2 continuous tool path smoothing algorithm for robotic machining with the consideration of redundant kinematics. A 5th degree PH spline is used to smooth the tool path position and orientation that satisfies C2 continuity. To achieve smooth and continuous motion, the redundant rotation angles are smoothed using a 5th degree polynomial spline as a function of the tool position

displacement. This polynomial spline is optimized to minimize global acceleration. Synchronizing the tool path position spline parameter, the tool orientation spline parameter, and the redundant angle spline parameter along the path are mapped to the tool tip position displacements. The global minimum time feed planning method considering the joint velocity, acceleration, and jerk constraints is adopted to interpolate motion commands. The algorithm effectively reduces abrupt motion changes and ensures a smoother movement of the robot. The proposed method shows a 33.97% higher motion efficiency compared to the regular PH spline method. Furthermore, experiments demonstrate that the proposed method effectively mitigates the joint vibration with a maximum reduction of 65.05%, without compromising motion accuracy.
