Constrained Sampling-Based MPC using Path Integral for Collision-Free Robot Manipulation
Xingfang Wang, Hui Li, Dong Wang, Xiao Huang, Zhihong Jiang
Abstract The dynamic and unknown human behaviors in human-robot interaction make it challenging for collision-free robot manipulation. Although sampling-based MPC has achieved real-time control in the above scenarios, it is hard to handle equality hard constraints, such as working along a speciﬁed trajectory, due to sampling disturbances. To improve manipulation performance under multiple constraints, this paper presents a novel constrained sampling-based MPC (CSMPC) method using path integral. Firstly, hierarchical optimization combining policy sampling projection and the Lagrange multiplier method is used to handle equality hard constraints for high-precision manipulation tasks. Secondly, collision avoidance and smooth motion are modeled as inequality soft constraints, where collision detection and time series prediction are used to ensure the safety and smoothness of dynamic interaction. Finally, an adaptive noise method is built to improve the stability of physical robot manipulation. The simulation and experiment results demonstrate that the proposed method enables a 7-DOF robot manipulator to achieve precise manipulation while avoiding dynamic obstacles.
Keywords collision avoidance, model predictive control, optimal control, robot manipulation, robotics and control systems, sampling-based planning

I. INTRODUCTION
W ITH the development of human-robot interaction, the work area of robots and humans is gradually moving from separation to integration. This poses a huge challenge to collision-free robot manipulation [1]. Because the robot must consider dynamic and unknown human behavior, along with various unstructured obstacles in real-time, as shown in Fig. 1. The robot needs to comprehensively solve the multi-constraint problems of collision avoidance, smooth motion, and various task requirements. Therefore, how to handle the above multiple constraints quickly and safely in a human-robot environment is a challenging decision and control problem.
The above problem in static scenarios has been well solved by many methods. However, when applied in actual dynamic scenarios, there are still some drawbacks in terms of computational cost and policy optimality. Operational Space Control (OSC) is a classical method to address such problems [2]. It

However, the process of inverse Kinodynamic in OSC typically incurs high computational costs and singularity, and this method cannot guarantee policy optimality in dynamic scenarios. Moreover, it is a model-based control method and inaccurate models can severely affect its control performance [3]. As popular sampling-based planning algorithms, Rapidly-exploring Random Trees Star (RRT*) [4] and Probabilistic RoadMap (PRM) [5] are also able to provide a fast solution for the static manipulation scenarios of multi-dimensional robot systems. However, the interference of dynamic obstacles may seriously affect the convergence of the algorithm [6]. With the development of artificial intelligence, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has been applied to robot manipulation. RL can complete many challenging tasks in non-structured environments by learning the end-to-end representation of the optimal policy [7], [8]. Although RL can reduce its dependence on model accuracy by learning the system model, it usually requires a lot of training time in practical applications, which affects its implementation in actual dynamic environments [9].
Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a promising algorithm framework for the dynamic and uncertain nature of the environment. And it has been successfully applied in practical scenarios such as autonomous driving [10], humanoid walking [11], and safe navigation in human-robot interaction [12]. MPC uses the system model to predict the system’s response over a future time horizon, and based on these predictions,

computes the optimal control policy to achieve a stable, robust, and high-performance control effect. Based on the use of gradients, MPC can be categorized into gradient-based MPC and sampling-based MPC, with the former directly utilizing gradients and the latter estimating gradients in optimization. Benefiting from the smooth dynamics model obtained by derivation, gradient-based MPC is suitable for handling multi-constraint optimization problems in high-dimensional systems. For example, the combination of MPC and Differential Dynamic Programming (MPC-DDP) enables the robot to perform tasks such as dual-arm coordination and mobile manipulation to avoid human interference [13]–[15]. In [11], an efficient hierarchical control framework is designed using MPC and iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (MPC-iLQR) to explore robust whole-body control of humanoid robots. However, gradient-based MPC methods require that the cost and dynamics are differentiable, making it difficult to handle discrete problems. Actual manipulation tasks often involve discontinuous phenomena, which increases the complexity of modeling and time to optimize convergence.
Sampling-based MPC, such as the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) and Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), provides a promising alternative approach [16], [17]. Sampling-based MPC can estimate gradients and solve discrete optimization problems by sampling. This method does not require differentiability, making its modeling simpler. According to [18], MPPI can also be viewed as an application of CEM on stochastic diffusion processes. It is based on the path integral control framework [19], and solves the optimization problem using importance sampling and Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, which are the key concepts of information theory. Sampling-based MPC methods do not require linear or quadratic approximations of cost, and dynamics, which can be simply and flexibly applied to robot manipulation tasks in dynamic environments [20], [21]. They construct inequality constraints by designing clamping functions in dynamics [10] or adding control barrier functions [22] and quadratic penalty terms [23] in the cost. However, limited by the stochasticity of samples, these methods have difficulties handling equality constraints. It is hard to meet the needs in precise robot manipulation, such as tracking the path for workpiece grinding and maintaining a stable motion posture during medical examinations in human-robot interaction scenarios. Moreover, these methods amplify the disadvantage of producing non-smooth motion trajectories in high-dimensional robot systems, leading to system instability [24].
A. Contribution
In this paper, we focus on enhancing the ability of samplingbased MPC using path integral to strictly handle equality constraints in high-dynamic environments. Furthermore, we aim to achieve smooth robot motion and satisfactory manipulation precision without violating equality constraints, ensuring the real-time, safety and accuracy of the collision-free robot manipulation. Specifically, this paper has the following contributions:
1) We propose a novel sampling-based MPC method that strictly handles equality constraints in stochastic op-

timization. Hierarchical optimization combining nullspace projection of policy sampling and the Lagrange multiplier method is used to provide a general derivation for state-only and state-input equality constraints, which can deal with equality hard constraints. 2) We develop a novel method to make collision avoidance and smooth motion as inequality soft constraints without violating equality constraints. Deep visual perception and robot skeleton models are used for collision detection of dynamic obstacles. The time series smoothing prediction method based on statistics is designed to suppress noise interference and ensure motion smoothness. 3) We provide a novel method to improve the motion stability for robot manipulation. Adaptive noise is able to adjust the exploration capability of sampling based on task performance, which can avoid exploration stagnation in optimization and improve smooth motion transition during collision-free manipulation.
The proposed method is verified by precise manipulation tasks in dynamic scenarios, including safe sorting of items, trajectory tracking, polishing workpiece holes, and contactbased medical examination. The structure of this paper is as follows. Section II outlines the preparatory work, Section III provides a detailed description of the proposed method, Sections IV and V present the simulation and experimental results, and Section VI concludes this paper.

III. CONSTRAINED SAMPLING-BASED MPC

We now propose the core method of this paper, as shown in Fig. 2. The adaptive noise method adjusts the noise amplitude Σ to enhance the exploration capability of sampling. Then, the equality constraints are handled to obtain the constrained sampling trajectories H. Inequality constraints related to collision avoidance and smooth motion are appropriately added to the trajectory cost C(V ), which is calculated based on H. Finally, the robot executes the result of the optimization update, and its state x0 is passed to the next optimization cycle. C. Inequality constraint: smooth motion
The stochasticity of the sampling process can cause obvious oscillations in the output results over time, increasing the burden on the actuators and leading to system instability. This is a common problem of sampling-based MPC. To address this problem, some authors propose to smooth the output control sequence by fitting local polynomial approximations to the control sequence [10]. Although this external smoothing method has achieved good results, it affects the optimality and destroys the equality constraint relationship of the output results. Recent works have also not considered the equality constraint relationship in smoothing processing [20], [24].

D. Adaptive noise

In the sampling process, the amplitude Σ determines the

range of noise, which in turn affects the quality of samples and

optimal results. Usually, an appropriate Σ can ensure a good

sampling performance in simple and certain tasks. However,

in the precise tasks of dynamic human-robot interaction, a

fixed Σ may not guarantee a good sampling exploration ability.

Because the exploration speed needs to be increased to ensure

optimization efficiency at the beginning of the task, and re-

duced to ensure optimization accuracy and control stability at

the end. Therefore, we propose a method that can dynamically

adjust the Σ according to the task execution situation, to

smoothly improve the exploration ability.

C. Comparative analysis
A comparative analysis is conducted between CSMPC and two popular MPC methods (OCS2 [13], STORM [20]), as shown in Table I. OCS2 is a gradient-based MPC toolbox developed by ETH Zurich, known for its efficient and stable capabilities that have been well-validated in precise manip-

Thus, these two methods are chosen as representative advanced methods of the two types of MPC for comparison with CSMPC. The comparison simulations are conducted under the same computing environment, where the CPU model is AMD Ryzen 5 3600X 6-Core Processor and the GPU model is NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1660.
Compared to OCS2, CSMPC achieves shorter optimization times under the same order of magnitude of constraint error. This is because the requirement of high-precision equality constraints reduces the optimal convergence speed of OCS2. Then, OCS2 constructs obstacle avoidance costs by defining the collision body list of the robot’s URDF model, which increases the optimization solving time compared to the proposed robot skeleton model approach. Moreover, as shown in Simulation 4, CSMPC can solve discrete optimization problems, which cannot be achieved by gradient-based MPC methods such as OCS2. Compared to STORM, CSMPC can handle equality constraints with high precision without violating inequality constraints. However, STORM needs to convert equality constraints into costs, which may not be compatible with obstacle avoidance costs in dynamic scenarios, leading to poor tracking effects or potential collisions in Simulations 2 and 3. Although the optimization time of CSMPC is slightly longer than STORM’s due to equality constraint matrix calculations, the manipulation error of CSMPC is 1000 times smaller than STORM’s. In Simulation 1, STORM may face exploration stagnation near the goal due to inappropriate sampling noise. However, CSMPC can solve this problem and improve reaching accuracy by at least ten times using the adaptive noise method. The motion smoothness of CSMPC is guaranteed by the smooth motion method, which can be seen in the video attachment. Therefore, CSMPC solves the problem that sampling-based MPC methods cannot accurately handle equality constraints, and its convergence speed is faster than that of the gradient-based MPC methods.

VI. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose a constrained sampling-based MPC (CSMPC) method based on the path integral control framework. The core content of this method is closely related to multi-constraint processing. Firstly, it provides an effective method for handling equality constraints during sampling,

including the processing of multiple or dynamical equality constraints. Secondly, it designs inequality constraints that ensure motion safety and smoothness, improving the control performance on high-dimensional robot systems without violating equality constraints. We apply CSMPC to a 7DOF robot to solve the problem of balancing interaction safety and manipulation efficiency. The experimental results show that based on deep visual perception, the robot can accurately perform tasks while avoiding various obstacles in dynamic environments, including moving people and objects of different shapes. Because of its ability to accurately and safely handle multiple constraints in dynamic environments, CSMPC can be a promising research direction not only in human-robot interaction, but also in robot-robot interaction and environment-robot interaction.
