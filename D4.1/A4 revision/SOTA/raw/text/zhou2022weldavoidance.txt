ABSTRACT
The offline path planning problem for arc welding robots becomes increasingly difficult to achieve with the increasing product complexity and the open work environment, such as the welding tasks in ship formation. Most research considers the path planning problem without time limitations or other constraints that conform to the actual production process. During the welding process, the placement of workpieces seriously affects the efficiency of finding feasible paths for the welding robots for weld tasks that cannot be repeated accurately. Thus, in the flow welding workshop, the improved lazy Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM) algorithm based online collisionfree path planning method is proposed to finish the welding tasks of general stiffened plates at an arbitrary position on the workbench within the robot’s reach. The work environment is built with the grid method to reduce the computational dimension of collision detection. The local path planner is implemented by the lazyPRM algorithm with the repulsive force field to rapidly search the collision-free path for the welding robot with specific safe distances. The robot posture adjustment strategy is also applied to the collision detection process to adjust some poses of the robot at candidate paths to be feasible. The improved path planning method is tested on both the simulated environment and actual application scenario and proved to be suitable for the path planning problem of the arc welding robot. The results of this study have been integrated into a computer approach, which can accelerate the motion planning process and reduce human efforts.

1. Introduction
In the past few decades, intelligent path planning attracts plenty of attention since the multi-degree-of-freedom (DOF) robots with high flexibility are widely used in industrial production and manufacturing to enhance the efficiency and quality of production, such as welding, painting, grinding, and assembling, etc. [1]. Traditional welding manufacturing based on offline planning commands welding robots to operate repetitive welding tasks on the workbench. However, as product complexity increases and the open working environment, the offline path planning of the welding robot becomes inapplicable to accomplish the arc welding tasks under specific requirements. For instance, in the welding tasks of shipbuilding, welding robots usually work in a relatively large area, and the working environment of the welding robot is always changing since the workpiece is placed at a slightly different position each time. Due to specific sizes and weights of stiffened plates, no fixture is required to fix the workpiece during the welding process, and the change of workpiece position and corresponding work

environment may cause the potential path planned offline in advance will collide. Besides, the path planning problem of welding robots, especially for high-dimensional manipulators, under the static working environment usually requires manual teaching or programming resulting in a heavy computation burden, time-consuming, low accuracy, and flexibility which is not suitable for commercial applications [2]. In this case, the online path planning of the welding robot in shipbuilding is an urgent problem to be solved to carry out a feasible collision-free path rapidly and complete the assigned tasks.
Path planning problem can be defined as the optimal collision-free trajectory planning scheme which moves the robot from the start point to the goal point under specific constraints. A well-known approach to solving offline collision-free path planning problems is the probabilistic sampling-based algorithm, which explores the configuration space using independently and identically distributed random samples instead of the explicit construction of the configuration space. The representative algorithms include the probabilistic roadmap (PRM) algorithm [3–6], the rapidly exploring random tree (RRT*) algorithm

[7–9], the fast matching tree (FMT*) algorithm [10,11], and Batch informed trees (BIT) algorithm [12], etc. And they have been proved to possess probabilistic completeness and asymptotically optimality. Moreover, other intelligent optimization methods such as genetic algorithm (GA) [13,14], (ACO) algorithm [15] and particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm [16] are introduced in the path planning problem to find an optimal collision-free path for the robot. The intelligent optimization methods aim to transform the path planning problems of the multi-DOF robot into an optimization problem and then find the optimal path by iterations. But, intelligent optimization algorithms have difficulties in efficiently solving the online path planning problem, such as slow convergence, hard to jump out of the local optima, and further affecting the solution search efficiency.
However, most algorithms have trouble utilizing the actual problems directly. Most methods simply abstract manipulators as lines, and collision is detected between lines or the tool center point (TCP) of the robot and obstacles without considering the body constraints of the robot during motion planning. Therefore, the modeling accuracy of the multi-DOF manipulator in collision detection will seriously affect the collision detection, resulting in the degradation of obstacle avoidance and path optimization capabilities of the algorithm. Yangmin Xie et al. [17] introduced an improved distorted configuration (DC) space method for a 3-DOF robot path planning considering the demands on automatic planning, convex space preservation and path optimization, where the automatic collapse point generation algorithm collapsed the obstacles in the configuration space, node growing algorithm was used to ensure the boundary and convex property of the distorted space. And the trajectory optimization method was added to shorten the path obtained from the DC-space approach. Gang Chen et al. [18] proposed an improved PRM algorithm with three stages to quickly plan a short and safe trajectory in the presence of obstacles in its workspace, especially for the workspace that contains narrow passages. A new sampling method and virtual force field were applied to the algorithm to construct the roadmap, and the connection strategy guarantees a high-connectivity roadmap. The algorithm was tested on 6-DOF and 8-DOF robots, and simulation results proved it was available for manipulators of any degree of freedom. Wenxiang Gao et al. [19] presented an offline collision-free motion planning algorithm by considering the task redundancy existing in manufacturing when the welding environment has a narrow passage or dense obstacles. In the proposed algorithm, the trajectory of the robot end-effector with specific poses was optimized by minimizing the torch angular cost, and the sampling strategy was developed to improve the search efficiency. Jingjing Xu et al. [20] provided a pseudo-distance algorithm for collision detection of manipulators to implement the motion control of the robot under both the static and dynamic obstacle environment with complex shapes. The convex-plane-polygons-based distance algorithm was applied to the collision-free trajectory planning of the robot to show its effectiveness on the robot motion control.
Although these algorithms perform well on collision-free path planning problems and have been improved in recent years, they are still not good at responding the situations if unexpected obstacles occur or the working environment changes. Therefore, path planning algorithms usually combine the artificial potential field (APF) methods [21] to solve the online path planning with obstacle avoidance, which could adjust the robot to move towards the target point and backward when near the obstacles based on the attractive and repulsive forces generated by APF, respectively. Gang Chen et al. [22] proposed a collision-free path planning method with two stages. Collision detection is accomplished by testing the intersection of the axis-aligned bounding box trees based on the established point-cloud model, while path planning is achieved to adjust the preplanned path by the D* algorithm in real-time. Besides, Zhihao Xu et al. [23] transformed the motion planning problem into quadratic programming (QP) problem considering the predefined task, physical constraints and feasible space constraints. Then they established the recurrent neural network to solve the QP problem online. Other methods also have been extensively studied, such as applying the

Robotics and Computer-Integrated Manufacturing 78 (2022) 102413
deep Q-Network algorithm to the path planning problem of robots for both static and dynamic environments [24], using the trained machine learning model to represent one of the redundant controllers in dynamic path planning problems [25], the combination of the electromagnetic field and deep neural networks [26] to solve the real-time path planning algorithm of robots. Such intelligent algorithms have better flexibility and scientificity than traditional methods, but they are mostly applied to robot trajectory planning which needs a significant amount of experimental data to train the model.
As described above, plenty of simulations have been done to research the welding path optimization [27], offline path planning of the multi-DOF robot [28,29], the online trajectory planning of industrial robots [30,31], few of them considered the complete processing of the welding tasks on industrial robots. In the actual shipbuilding welding workshop, different workpieces need to be processed in turn, which means the robot has limited time to plan a collision-free transition path after scanning the current workpiece and working environment. Because the placement of the workpiece is random within the reachable workspace of the welding robot at each processing, it is challenging since the approach must be highly efficient and accurate to solve the path planning problem under a changing environment that satisfies the specific motion constraints. Fortunately, the digital model of each workpiece is known before processing, so the relative offset distance and rotation amount of the workpiece can be obtained by the infrared laser scanning, and the corresponding model in the simulation program can be adjusted immediately to improve the calculation accuracy and reduce the computation cost. This operation also facilitates rapid planning of collision-free paths and enhances the efficiency of the welding robot. Once the workpiece position changes, the work environment is adjusted immediately, and the path is quickly replanned.
As a result, a PRM framework-based path planning method with the repulsive field strategy is proposed in this paper to generate a collisionfree welding transition path as soon as possible in the open work environment. Compared with other existing welding robot path planning researches, the contributions are listed as follows:
• An automatic path planning algorithm is proposed for collision-free path planning. The proposed algorithm aims to avoid collisions and reduce the computing time of collision detection based on both the repulsive field and the lazy-PRM framework [32]. Additionally, the improved random sampling method helps the algorithm find feasible paths with a relatively high probability in the case of a small number of sampling points. The detailed content is described in Section 3.
• The robot posture adjustment strategy is also applied to the algorithm when the end-effector collides with obstacles, which can slightly adjust the pose of the end-effector and add new transition points to increase the feasibility of paths.
• The online path detection and replanning method is introduced to deal with the path planning problem in a different environment with different welding workpieces. The laser scanning sensor can be utilized to capture the position of the workpiece to help the algorithm adjusts the simulated environment, check the feasibility of predefined paths, and replan the path if it is infeasible.
The improvements in the algorithms and strategies are necessary in order to better cope with the collision-free path planning problems of welding robots in the changing environment. PRM is selected to search for feasible paths compared rapidly according to the characteristics of the workpiece and the real-time requirements of the welding task. The main reason is that the random sampling technique based PRM has few parameters and a simple structure that can effectively solve the path planning problems in high dimensional space and complex constraints. Meanwhile, the probability graph obtained by random sampling can be used repeatedly once established, and the time of building probability graphs for the same workpiece with different positions is greatly saved. The performance still needs to be improved due to the randomness of

2. Problem description
The path planning problem studied in this paper focuses on finding the transition path of the 7-DOF welding robot from the initial position through each welding seam and finally back to the initial position. The welding paths of the robot are usually fixed and not considered. The purpose of this work is to quickly plan an optimal and collision-free transition path of the robot in the simulation software based on the actual scene, and then transmit the obtained path information from the host computer to the robot controller so that to drive the robot to finish the arc welding tasks. The whole workspace is shown in Fig. 1, where the working robot is a 7-DOF welding robot since it has an external shaft. The robot used in this paper is FANUC M20iD/25, and the initial robot position and the user coordinate O are predefined and shown in Fig. 1. The workpieces applied in the problem are mutually perpendicular stiffened plates. The infrared laser sensor fixed on the bracket is directly above the workbench, which is used to scan the position of the workpiece as the robot moves. The welding gun is equipped with a weld tracking sensor to improve the welding accuracy. But it also limits the welding angle and direction of the torch and increases the difficulty of path planning.
The whole process of the approach is described in Fig. 2. All operations during welding path planning are accomplished through the host computer without manual adjustment. 3. Methodology

This section focuses on the whole process of the proposed approach, which consists of three stages: the offline preparation before searching the transition paths, the online work environment adjustment and the query stage of feasible paths. The complete flow diagram of the proposed method is described in Fig. 3. Offline preparation aims to build the

Online adjustment mainly adjusts the position of the constructed workpieces and the corresponding roadmaps to fit the actual environment. 3.1. Offline preparation
3. 1.1 Modelling Before searching the feasible paths for the arc welding robot, several
things need to be finished, such as the virtual environment construction, the welding sequence, and robot postures at the terminals of each welding seam. In order to accurately build the virtual environment that is consistent with the actual scene in software, the model of the welding robot and workpieces can be established using the three-dimensional grid method according to the obtained STL files. The grid size is uniformly set as 10 mm for the robot with a welding gun and the workpiece. The space is divided into small cubes with specific grid sizes, and each cube is represented by the vertex that approaches positive infinity. Vertices containing the environmental obstacles such as the workpiece or robot will be recorded in the archive, and the vertex set is regarded as the obstacle space. 3.1.2. Environment construction The grid modeling method defines the girds that are not fully filled
with modeling objects as obstacle space, then the space occupied by modeling objects is expanded. Welding seams on the corner surface of the workpiece will sink into the grids always located in the obstacle space, resulting in the impossibility of finding feasible paths. According to the geometric characteristics of the perpendicular stiffened plates, a motion planning rule based on practice is introduced, as shown in Fig. 5. The robot can preferentially move to the top of the workpiece, and the algorithm then adjusts the welding posture and plans feasible paths for the robot to avoid collisions occurring near the workbench. Thus, the actual welding seams are replaced by the approximate transition points that gradually increment the distance along the vertical direction of the welding seam and then along the tool axis-z. And the error of the path created by the transition point in this part could be automatically adjusted by the weld tracking device. In addition, the posture of the welding robot on the welding point is different in various welding sequences and directions due to the restriction factor that the welding tracking device must be directly in front of the welding gun movement direction, so the welding sequence and direction need to be determined in advance. For instance, the welding gun moves forward in the welding direction from WP2 to WP1, as Fig. 5 shows. If the welding direction changes to WP1-WP2, the welding gun will rotate 180◦ around itself and move backward. Also, the end of the welding gun is perpendicular to the welding seam, and the angle between the welding gun and two stiffened platers is set to 45◦ The practical workpieces are relatively simple, and the welding sequence with corresponding seam directions can be directly assigned. If the workpiece is complex with many welding seams, it can be achieved by applying the heuristic algorithms.

cover the whole free space at a certain number of sampling points. Therefore, improving the sampling process could effectively enhance the performance of the sampling-based path planner. 3.4. Collision detections
Collision detection aims to detect whether the robot collides with the workpiece, the workbench and the main body itself. During the welding process, welding seams are known and fixed, and the robot is considered collision-free during its journey along this path. The transition path of the robot that moves from the destination of one welding seam to the starting point of the other should be planned and detect collisions, while the detailed process is displayed in Fig. 8. After obtaining the feasible paths from the improved A*, each transition path is separated into Q interpolating points by the piecewise sampling method, and collision detection is performed on the robot pose at each interpolating point. According to the grid method, the robot poses represented by the set of grids vertices at these interpolating points. In the detection process, the vertices set of the robot and the obtained grid vertices set of the workpiece are compared to check whether intersection point(s) exists. If the intersection point(s) exists, it means an overlapping area is generated between the robot and obstacles, and the detected transition path is determined as collisional and should be eliminated. Under the circumstances, the robot posture adjustment strategy is triggered for a maximum of 20 times when the collision occurs between the welding gun and the workpiece to update the planned path and back to the collision detection stage; otherwise, the path transition point and the corresponding path connection are eliminated from the roadmap, and the path is replanned. If there is no overlapping part, the path is feasible, and the path information can be output to control the robot finishing the welding task through the host communication.

3.5. Posture adjustment strategy
Most paths are eliminated when collisions occur between the main body of the robot and the workpiece(s), which may cause the known information loss of paths and the work environment. For instance, the robot position can be obtained from welding points, transition points and other information in the robot path planning process, while its postures cannot be predicted or defined in advance except for two terminals of the entire path. In particular, the poses of the welding gun heavily affect the feasibility of the path when only the welding gun collides with the workpiece. Generally, the impropriate welding gun posture will affect the path to be judged as infeasible. But it is possible to make the path feasible if fine-tuning the pose toward the welding gun. On behalf of reducing the waste of computing resources, improving the success rate of collision detection and the efficiency of the path search, the posture adjustment strategy for the robot is employed only when collision detection is failed between the welding gun and workpiece. Besides, the adjustment process is only used during the transition path search and affects the welding posture and weld quality.
Once the posture adjustment strategy is triggered, it is necessary to find the appropriate direction that the welding gun should move to

adjust the position and posture of the robot, where the detailed process is described in Fig. 9. 4.2. Simulation results
In two scenarios where the starting and goal points are set differently with specific robot poses, the simulation results for each algorithm are listed in table 2, which presents the general parameter settings and the obtained average values with variance in the bracket for each algorithm. The best trajectories obtained from RRT*, AB-RRT*, lazy-PRM and improved lazy-PRM are displayed in Fig. 10, respectively. The obstacles are displayed as four lavender cubes, transition points are marked as magenta, the start and goal points are set to red and green, and the planned trajectories are drawn in orange.
According to the table, it can be seen that the proposed improved lazy-PRM can achieve the best performance in scenario 1 and the best average pathlength and cost in scenario 2 at the number of samples with 600, maximum repulsion level 13 and penalty parameter μ = 1. The step size for RRT* and AB-RRT* are set to 200 mm since the defined workspace is quite large. And the search process is finished as long as no collision occurs between the searched sampling point and the goal point to improve efficiency. The best minimal path length of AB-RRT* in scenario 2 indicates a strong search capability, but the random search strategy results in low stability. Besides, AB-RRT* takes the most time to find feasible paths since the bidirectional path search policy and the robot posture adjustment strategy increase the number of collision detection, and the resulting average path length is longer than other algorithms. In contrast, the lazy-PRM based algorithms take relatively little time on a few numbers of collision detection to finish the searching. Especially for the improved algorithm, which maintains stable and high performance on finding collision-free paths within the acceptable time. As for the lazy-PRM algorithm, a longer time is taken than the proposed algorithm to build the roadmap. It is mainly because the proposed sampling method could generate more uniformly distributed sample points with higher probabilities for connecting the roadmap than the traditional random sampling method. The repulsive force field is applied to the proposed algorithm to select the best path with a safe distance between the welding robot and obstacles instead of the shortest path, and the posture adjustment strategy uses the collision information to adjust the obtained candidate path to be feasible, which further reduce the number of collision detection and the path search time. The total cost comparison of the three algorithms also proves that the proposed algorithm achieves the best overall performance.
In Fig. 10, each plot shows the robot posture at the goal point, which means the joint configuration obtained from different algorithms are not the same although the goal position and the pose of the welding gun are given, and the paths obtained by algorithms also have differences.

Although AB-RRT* achieves the shortest path length in scenario 2, the obtained trajectory is not smooth and may affect the robot’s motion. And the transition points saved by RRT* during the search seem similar to AB-RRT*, where the advantages of bidirectional growing trees are not obvious. The proposed algorithm almost founds the best paths in both scenarios, especially for the path obtained on scenario 2, which can be approximated to a smooth curve. Besides, it can be seen from the path information of the proposed algorithm in Fig. 10(a) that the second transition point is obtained after the robot posture adjustment strategy. The robot pose is updated after the posture adjustment strategy, and the adjusted path becomes collision-free. It is obvious that the obstacles can be well avoided during the robot motion, which indicates the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed improved PRM method.

To clearly understand the robot movement process, the first 6 subplots display the trajectories of the welding robot at different joints, and the last one exhibits the entire movement of the welding robot. The movement of the base joint displays the robot moves as the external axis moves. It can be seen that the motion of each joint is within a small acceptable range, which makes the path shorter and smoother.

The reason for this result is that the time spent on building the roadmap increases rapidly when more sampling points are adopted to the algorithm, and more options in the roadmap to construct a feasible path with a shorter path length. The repulsion level is used to measure the safe distance between the welding robot and obstacles, where the maximum safe distance can be calculated by multiplying the grid size and the repulsion level. The collision between the welding gun and obstacles is the most urgent issue to be avoided in the arc welding robot path planning problem. Therefore, the main purpose of the repulsion level is to measure the range of the safe distance from the welding gun center to obstacles. According to the actual size of the welding gun, we set the maximum repulsion level within the range [10, 15]. Because the welding gun collides with obstacles in any posture if the repulsion level is less than 10, which increases the time of path search, and it hardly collides with obstacles, and the obtained path will have a longer path length if the repulsion level exceeds 15. In this case, an appropriate repulsion level also could balance the contradiction between finding the shortest path and the time consumption of path search. As a result, it is found that the algorithm has the minimum total cost at a repulsion level of 13 through table 4.
In table 5, the effectiveness of the penalty parameter μ is tested and demonstrated through the proposed evaluations. μ is positively correlated with time consumption and path length. The entire searching time and the path length become lower as μ reduces. It is due to the penalty distance in Eq. (9) being used to measure the safe distance between the welding robot and obstacles. The penalty distance becomes larger when applying a larger value of μ, which causes the searched path with a higher fitness value is less likely to be selected for collision detection. And vice versa. According to the data shown in table 5, it can be observed that the reduction in total cost value is slight as μ is less than 6, and μ = 1 achieves the best overall performance on the proposed algorithm. If μ = 0, which means the repulsive force field is invalid in the algorithm, and the feasible path is difficult to be found within the limited time due to failures of collision detection.

6. Conclusion and future work
To plan a collision-free path for a high-DOF welding robot efficiently, the improved lazy-PRM method is proposed in this paper to solve the online collision-free path planning problem for the arc welding robot. The proposed approach consists of three main stages. In the offline preparation stage, the work environment is constructed by the grid method, which greatly reduces the computing consumption in a higher dimensional environment. And the improved sampling strategy expanded the space for connecting routes when building roadmaps,

14

X. Zhou et al.
which improved the probability of finding feasible paths. The online adjustment stage aims to map the actual situation to the simulated environment, including the coordinate system unification between the welding robot, workbench and workpieces. And the purpose of the query stage is to search for the shortest collision-free paths over a shorter period of time, which requires fewer collision detection times and safer distances between the robot and obstacles. In this case, the repulsive force field is applied to the algorithm with the improved cost function that contains the information on safe distances and is combined with the lazy-PRM strategy to achieve the goals of online planning. From the theoretical perspective, the work proposes an automatic process of the online path planning problem for the arc welding robot. The proposed method proves its effective and superior performance on several tests from the simulated environment and the actual welding process. It also could replace manual teaching and give guidance for automatic welding tasks.
Future work will concern more with improving the welding production efficiency of the actual welding process. On the one hand, the welding sequence optimization should be improved with algorithms rather than manual definition if the welding seams in a workpiece become abundant and complex. And it is worth studying the welding sequence planning when multiple workpieces are placed on the workbench simultaneously since the current approach mainly considers the welding of a single workpiece. On the other hand, the reachable space of a single robot is not enough to cover the entire workbench sometimes, which may need two or more robots to finish the welding process. In this case, the cooperative path planning problem for the welding robot also has potential for future research.
