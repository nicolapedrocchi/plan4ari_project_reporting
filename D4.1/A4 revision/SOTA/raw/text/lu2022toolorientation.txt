High-order joint-smooth trajectory planning method considering tool-orientation constraints and singularity avoidance for robot surface machining
Lei Lu a, Lei Zhang a, Cheng Fan a,*, Hao Wang b,*

ABSTRACT
Compared with the traditional machine tools, robots are low-cost and flexible for the machining process. However, owing to the complex constraints in the operation space and the redundant degrees of freedom (DoFs), it is challenging to obtain a joint-smooth optimal trajectory for the robotic machining. This article develops a high-order smooth joint trajectory planning method for robot surface machining considering with the tool posture constraints (the collision avoidance and cutting angles limits) and the robot state limits (the singularity avoidance). In the proposed method, the whole trajectory of the tool posture is integrated by the optimized differential vector in steps. In every integrating step, the differential vector is optimized from the redundant DoFs with the combined and balanced optimization objective considering the joint-smooth requirements, tool orientation limits and robot state constraints. At the same time, the upper limit of the second-order differential is given to limit the change rate of the optimized differential vector and to smooth the generated trajectory further. As the drastic change of the feasible region and tool-tip path curve, the boundary of the feasible region should be smoothed in advance to compensate for the hysteresis of the numerical integration method. In the end, two cases of robot surface machining are provided to verify the method. In the experimental process, an UR-5 robot with Robot Operating System (ROS) controller is used and the complex curve is machined by the robot.

1. Introduction
Owing to the low cost and flexibility, the robots show an increasing share in machining the components with complex structures and freeform surfaces [1–4]. However, the nonlinear kinematics relationship for the robot and the complex structure of the workpiece make it difficult to find the optimal machining process [5–7].
Compared with the conventional motion planning of the 6R robot, the joint trajectory planning for robot surface machining is much more challenging [8–10]. Firstly, it is a fundamental and tricky problem for 6R robot motion planning when the dimension of the operation space is reduced by constraints [11–13]. For the surface machining process, the main constraints in the operation space are the tool tip needs to follow the tool path curve strictly [14,15]. Secondly, the constrained space is further limited by cutting process requirements. The cutting angle requirements require the tool axis vector in a specific cone and on one side

of the feed direction in the operation space [16,17], and the potential mechanical collision between the tool holder, workpiece and robots also provides limits for the tool orientation space [18,19]. In the end, for most 6R serial robots, the singularity avoidance is important for the joint trajectory planning, as there is a drastic change or jump of the joint motion even for a smaller motion for the end effector of the robot in the operation space [20–22].
To tackle these motion planning problems for the robotic machining, the sampling-based method is an efficient way to find the trajectory in the joint space. Moreover, the Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL) provides a collection of state-of-the-art sampling-based motion planners. The planner explores the high-dimensional constrained configuration space with collision limits [23] and links the sampled valid robot states to obtain the available motion. However, in the majority of cases, the motion process found by the normal sampling-based algorithms is not optimal [24]. Sertac, Karaman [25] introduced PRM* (Probabilistic

To generate the trajectory of the robot machining process for the complex workpiece, many pieces of research have been reported to avoid collision and achieve a geometry-smooth machining process. Peng

et al. [30] used sequential linearization programming to improve the initial solution provided by the conventional graph-based method to obtain the smoothness trajectory from the redundant DoFs. Lu et al. [31] applied B-spline for the joint configuration interpolation. They presented an optimization method to solve the inverse kinematics problem of the 6R robot to follow a given tool path considering smoothness and collision-free requirements. However, only one redundant freedom, i.e., the rotation along its own axis for the cutting tool, was considered in these methods. But at a certain tool path trajectory point, the three degrees of freedom of the tool orientation for the ball-end surface machining process are free and the tool axis can swing in a defined area. The three degrees of freedom of the rotation movement of the ball-end cutting tool could refer to the redundant degrees of freedom compared with the translation movement. Liao et al. [32] optimized the redundant DoFs to obtain the higher stiffness robot posture by dividing the surface into multiple sub-regions. Lu et al. [33] also optimized the differential vector and integrated the whole posture trajectory by the optimized differential vector. But the change rate of the optimized differential vector is not limited and the singularity and collision avoidances are not to be considered in the method. Although singularity avoidance has been studied for the tool path planning in the five-axis surface machining [34,35], very few articles discussed singularity avoidance problems in the robotic-surface machining process.
In this article, a second-order limited joint trajectory generation method is developed for the surface machining by the robot with the tool posture and robot state constraints. (“High-order” in the paper mainly refers to the second-order differential vector is limited in the numerical integrating process.) In the method, the tool posture trajectory is integrated by using the differential vector, and the differential vector is optimized from the redundant DoFs in every integrating step with the combined optimization objective. In the differential vector optimizing process, the minimum of the vector is preferred, and the vector should also be optimized to have the trend to let the robot move away from the singularity point of the robot and to make the cutting tool axis move away from the tool orientation's boundary. At the same time, the change rate of the differential vector is also limited, i.e., the derivatives of the differential vector are limited to further smooth the trajectory. In the end, two tool-tip paths are provided to verify the joint trajectory planning method by using the UR-5 robot model and one of cases is machined on an aluminum part by the UR robot.

3.2. Singularity avoidance requirements

When the robot moves to the accessible boundary or the joints axis are collinear, the robot is in the singularity state. Its Jacobi matrix becomes a singularity matrix. At that point or near the singularity point, a small change in the end effector, the joints will change largely. In the machining process, the singularity state should be avoided. In mathematics, the condition number is often used to evaluate the singularity of the matrix. The condition number of the Jacobi matrix is defined as κ(q) = ⃦⃦J(q) ∞ − 1 ‖J(q) ‖∞ ⃦⃦ (4)

According to the definition of the condition number, the value range of the condition number is

1 ≤ κ(J) ≤ ∞

(5)

When the κ(J) is slightly larger than one, the Jacobi matrix J is wellconditioned, which means the robot state is far away from the singularity point. If the κ(J) is very large, the Jacobi matrix is said to be illconditioned and the robot is almost singular. When the robot state is at the singularity point, the condition number is equal to infinity. Therefore, the minimum of the condition number κ(J) of the robot's Jacobi matrix is one of the optimization objectives in the method to realize the singularity avoidance.
According to the proposed method, the differential move D is the optimized variables. Then, the differential move D should be optimized to let the robot move away from the singularity point, i.e., let the robot move to the state with the low condition number κ(J). To describe and prove the trajectory planning method, the joints' trajectory of two curves (one is a square curve and another is a complicate butterfly curve on an inclined surface) are planned by the method. The complicated butterfly curve on an inclined surface is machined in the practical machining process on an aluminum part. 7. Conclusion
Considering the collision requirements and the redundant DoFs, a high-order smooth joint trajectory planning method is provided for the robotic surface machining. In this method, the smoothness requirements, i.e., the minimal of the differential vector in the joint space and the trend of letting the tool axis move away from the feasible region's boundary and singularity points are set as the optimization objectives. To get the high-order smooth joint trajectory, the change rate of the optimized differential vector is limited in the numerical integrating process. Due to the hysteresis in the numerical integration process, the pre-smooth and pre-adjustment algorithms for the tool orientation boundaries are provided. In the end, the joint trajectories for two cases are planned by this method. In contrast with the other methods, the method can deal with the abrupt change of the tool orientation constraints and {LCS}, and improve the smoothness of the machining process. The feedrate scheduling results illustrate the effectiveness of the high-order smoothness and the collision avoidance is testified by tool path curve with blocks. The singularity avoidance is also verified and illustrated by Case A. In the end, the inclined butterfly curve is machined in practice on an aluminum part by the UR robot.
