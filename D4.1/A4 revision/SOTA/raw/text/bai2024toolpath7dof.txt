Abstract—Milling or grinding complex curved parts with multi-DOF (degree of freedom) robots has the flexibility advantage of flexible obstacle avoidance and consistent attitude machining. However, in surface machining, only a few degrees of freedom are usually required, such as 3-DOF in ball end milling, and 4-DOF are usually required in side milling. There are redundant degrees of freedom in machining with multi-degrees of freedom robots, and redundant degrees of freedom also provide optimization space for machining process trajectory planning. A 7-DOF robot machining system is composed of a rotating worktable and a 6-DOF robot. This paper proposes an optimization method for complex surface machining joint trajectories using 7-DOF robots, based on a sampling motion planning algorithm. According to machining process requirements, the tool point was constrained to the established machining trajectory, the tool axis vector is limited to a specific area. Finally, using the sampled motion planning method in the Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL) to derive and generate a trajectory that meets the task requirements, the total joint rotation is reduced by 12.4% compared to traditional trajectory planning methods.
Keywords-complex surface machining, redundant degree of freedom, motion planning, trajectory optimization
I. INTRODUCTION
With the development of product personalization and flexible technology, the application scenarios of industrial robots have increased significantly [1]. When the robot is used for spraying [2], polishing [3] and other operations, by combining the rotation of the spindle and the movement of the other five joints, the task can be completed with high efficiency and precise control. However, the redundant degrees of

freedom allows robots to have greater adaptability and flexibility [4]. Lu et al. [5] proposed a tool path optimization method for 6-DOF robot surface machining based on sampling motion planning algorithm to minimize the motion distance of all joints. Weingartshofer et al. [6] introduce a path planning framework that incorporates tolerances and redundancy to precisely optimize joint paths with specific goals and constraint designs. Liu et al. [7] designed a 6-axis robot with a 3-axis actuator for hub polishing. We propose an automatic joint motion planning method for a nine-axis industrial robot to achieve the shortest processing time.
When dealing with motion planning problems of highdimensional robots, sample-based motion planning is an effective method. There are two types of planners: graph-based planners [8] and tree-based planners [9]. Although when the number of samples increases without limit, the probability of finding a solution converges to 1 [10], in the case of multiple solutions, finding the optimal solution has become a necessary consideration. Karaman [11] introduced two new algorithms, PRM* and RRT*, which have been verified to have asymptotic optimality, that is, the solution cost they generate gradually approaches the optimal value over time.
In this paper, a rotating worktable is combined with a 6DOF robot to increase redundant degrees of freedom, and the tool tip point is successively restricted to the machining trajectory point, and the tool axis vector is restricted to a specific area. Finally, PRM* algorithm is used to process the trajectory points that meet the task requirements.

V. CONCLUSIONS In this paper, a motion model combining rotating worktable and 6-DOF robot is established. The spiral trajectory is taken as the tool trajectory, the constraint of the tool tip on the trajectory point is transformed into an equality function, the rotation

range of the tool axis is transformed into an inequality constraint, and the joint movement distance is taken as the optimization objective. The sampling based path planning method was used to optimize the machining process. On the basis of the obtained optimization results, the corresponding weight coefficients were added to the initial position and end position of each joint Angle, which further improved the optimization effect. The total motion length of the seven joints was reduced from 7.141rad to 6.25525rad, which was reduced by 12.4% compared with the traditional planning method.
