Abstract In a multi-robot manufacturing system, several robotic manipulators can be physically coupled and collaborate on a process along a given path. Due to the high degrees of freedom and the high-dimensional constraints for path tracking and physical coupling, it remains challenging to plan the motion trajectories for the robots toward the maximization of their collective capabilities, such as load capacity and stiffness. The state-of-the-art methods face conﬂicts between the optimality and computational complexity, as well as between constraint satisfaction and convergence speed. To resolve the conﬂicts, this paper proposes a single-stage optimization method set in order to solve for the optimal robot placement and the joint motion simultaneously under a given manufacturing path on the work-piece. This method set is centered upon a reduced Hessian optimization method that descends orthogonally to all the path and coupling constraints in a robot-speciﬁc null-space of reduced dimension, while also considering the motion limits. The convergence of the reduced Hessian method is proved mathematically. Furthermore, we verify the reduced Hessian method statistically against existing methods in numerical experiments of pulling a spring. To make the algorithm applicable to real-world robot-driven manufacturing processes with paths of arbitrary geometric complexity, the method set is extended with a parallel computing technique that deals with multiple connected path segments. Through real-world multi-segment milling experiments, we validate the concept of capability enhancement for the multi-robot manufacturing system under the usage of the proposed method set.

Keywords path planning for manipulators, dual arm manipulation, cooperative manipulation, optimization-based motion planning, constrained motion planning, loop-closure constraints, trajectory-placement planning

Introduction
Robotic manipulators are utilized in various manufacturing processes thanks to their ﬂexibility and versatility (Perzylo et al., 2019). In robot-driven 3D-printing, laser cutting, and welding, a high accuracy should be achieved on a predeﬁned path; in metal bending, robots should possess high load capacity; in milling processes, high path accuracy and high stiffness under large process forces are required (Abele et al., 2006). In these manufacturing processes, paths are pre-given which deﬁne the relative movement of a tool to a work-piece. Robots should manipulate either the tool or the work-piece to realize the pre-deﬁned relative movement. This relative movement may constrain all three translational and up to three rotational dimensions, but it speciﬁes neither

the absolute movement of robots, nor their placement to the work-piece. Consequently, no start or end conﬁgurations are given, which prohibits the use of point-to-point trajectory generation methods. To make the robots execute the relative movement, both the placement and the joint trajectories of the robots should be planned within their reachable limits. Meanwhile, capabilities of robots such as accuracy, load

When maximal achievable capabilities of a single robot are still insufﬁcient for a given task, a multi-robot system with physical coupling can be created. In the research ﬁeld of cooperative manipulation, it has been shown that the load bearing capability of the multi-robot system is enhanced if multiple robots form a physical coupling by grasping a common object (Erhart and Hirche, 2016). We are willing to optimize these capabilities further toward the limits to leverage the full potentials of the physically coupled robots. Such an idea is illustrated in the two steps in Figure 2. Since the capabilities of robots are highly dependent on the joint conﬁgurations (Chiu, 1988), the redundant degree of freedom (DOF) in placement and joint conﬁgurations should be exploited for the optimization. As such, the problem setting of a path-constrained trajectory planning algorithm is the optimization of redundant DOFs toward the maximization of a conﬁguration-dependent capability of the multi-robot system, while satisfying the physical coupling and path following constraints, as well as the motion limits.
The analysis of the state-of-the-art shows that the problem of multi-robot trajectory planning with path and coupling constraints is not solved with high performance. Most trajectory planning methods return sub-optimal results, because they do not exploit the redundant DOFs in the placement variables (Kalakrishnan et al., 2011; Zucker et al., 2013). Otherwise, the redundant DOFs are optimized separately, facilitating a complicated multi-level optimization problem (Nicola et al., 2018). To deal with the high-

Related work
In this section, the focus is on the solution approaches for trajectory planning and optimization for high DOF multirobot systems. Especially, the suitability for manufacturing processes is analyzed.
Basic approaches for trajectory planning
The two most common approaches for trajectory planning are based on sampling and optimization, respectively.
Sampling-based approach. Methods belonging to the sampling-based approach start from a given conﬁguration and search for collision-free via-points toward a goal conﬁguration or goal set. Along these via-points, path and coupling constraint functions deﬁne a constraint manifold within the conﬁguration space, as described in Sintov et al. (2018) and Kingston et al. (2019). The sampling is conﬁned to the constraint manifold by either projection- or continuation-based methods. In projection-based sampling methods, a point is originally sampled in the conﬁguration space without constraints by a general method such as Probabilistic Roadmap (Kavraki et al., 1996) or Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (Jaillet and Porta, 2012; LaValle and Kuffner Jr., 2001). This point is then projected back to the constraint manifold, as in Han (2001) and Kingston et al. (2019). In continuation-based methods, the locally approximated tangent space of the constraint manifold is constructed, where the sampling takes place (Yakey et al., 2001).
In the basic problem setting of these sampling-based methods, collision avoidance and ﬁnding the shortest path, instead of the enhancement of robots’ capabilities, are the focus, which are not aligned with the objective of the present paper. Moreover, the starting and ending points are determined separately (possibly optimized) prior to the sampling of via points in most samplingbased methods such as Diaz Posada et al. (2017); Lu et al. (2021). Determining different parts of decision variables in different stages makes the ﬁnal result suboptimal. Speciﬁcally, the optimization of, for example, the starting point is less meaningful for manufacturing processes as the robots might only start to but not fully engage the tool into the work-piece. At the following via points, however, the robots need a higher capability after the full engagement of the tool, but are already moving away from the optimal conﬁguration occupied by the

Optimization-based approach. Representative optimizationbased methods with path and coupling constraints include Stochastic Trajectory Optimization for Motion Planning (Kalakrishnan et al., 2011), Covariant Hamiltonian Optimization for Motion Planning (Zucker et al., 2013), and Sequential Convex Optimization (Bonalli et al., 2019; Schulman et al., 2014). However, these methods assume a pre-given starting conﬁguration and a ﬁxed placement, wasting the valuable DOFs for optimization. A few other optimization-based methods given as follows do leverage these DOFs by means of sampling, but still do not integrate them as decision variables of optimization. For 6-axis robots with a ﬁxed placement, up to eight discrete starting conﬁgurations are acquired in De Maeyer et al. (2017). For robots with additional redundant joints in Tang et al. (2023), the angles of redundant joints are sampled in their movement range to ﬁnd the conﬁgurations with the highest end-effector stiffness. In Weingartshofer et al. (2023), after sampling the possible placements and the ﬁrst point on the path, a set of feasible initial conﬁgurations is obtained by solving the analytical inverse kinematics (AIK). Starting from this set, optimization problems toward the second way-points, from the second to the third, and so on are solved by parallel computing. These methods that sample the redundant DOFs have the common draw-back of a lack of possibility to further adjust the previously sampled DOFs in the phase of optimization. Consequently, a large number of samples are taken. For complicated manufacturing paths, those samples that later lead to nonreachable path points cannot be adjusted but only be discarded. As such, these methods suffer from a low scalability w.r.t. number of redundant DOFs and a low success rate.
Different from the above, the multi-stage optimization method in Kabir et al. (2021) optimizes the whole joint trajectories of all robots, including their starting points. This is done by parameterizing the trajectories with B-splines and taking all the control points as decision variables for an optimization problem. However, at least one initial solution of the joint trajectory must be obtained, whose feasibility is difﬁcult to ensure in a highly constrained multi-robot system for complicated paths. Moreover, the multistage method requires more tuning efforts in multiple stages, and exhibits a low computational efﬁciency (14.6 s for one stage and 15 way-points in Kabir et al. (2021)).
Furthermore, the optimization-based approach has the common problem of running into local optima, which is

Handling path and coupling constraints
In the optimization-based planning approach, simply giving constraint equations on discretized trajectory points to an optimization solver does not always work (leading to a success rate of only 33.26% in our test case). This is because of the nonlinearity of the robot kinematics in constraint equations, as well as the lack of control of trajectories between the discretized points. The following methods exist to mitigate those issues.
Manifold operations. The constraint equations of coupling and path tracking for multiple robots deﬁne manifolds in the joint space. When all constraints are satisﬁed, the joint motion stays within the corresponding constraint manifold. If this constraint manifold is endowed with a metric deﬁning the distance between two points on it, one can leverage efﬁcient operations of the Riemannian geometry, namely the exponential and logarithmic maps, as well as parallel transport (Pennec et al., 2006). Equipped with these operations, the manifold optimization methods such as Newton-Raphson, Conjugate Gradient, and Trust Region are highly efﬁcient with available toolboxes (Boumal, 2023; Boumal et al., 2014). The manifold optimization has been utilized in parameter tuning within non-Euclidean spaces, such as learning impedance control parameters or optimizing the end-effector orientation for manipulability (Jaquier et al., 2020; Jaquier and Rozo, 2020). But path constraints inherently possess neither a Riemannian metric nor the efﬁcient operations. In Zucker et al. (2013), a projection similar to the exponential map (without logarithmic map) is conducted in every iteration of trajectory planning to move the decision variables back to the constraint manifold after an unconstrained step. However, without being directed by the logarithmic map near the optimal trajectory, the unconstrained step might be nearly orthogonal to the constraint manifold, which leads to a smaller optimization progress and

causes chatters that has to be constantly canceled out. This impedes the convergence speed of the optimization.
Penalty functions. Beside the actual goal of enhancing capabilities in the manufacturing process such as stiffness, the objective function is already populated with terms to penalize abrupt joint angle changes, singularity, and collision with the environment. However, adding the path and coupling constraints into the objective function as penalty terms is still a common practice as in, for example, Kalakrishnan et al. (2011), Schulman et al. (2014), and Kabir et al. (2019). Cost weight scheduling, as proposed in Zucker et al. (2013), is thus indispensable. This has been developed into the penalty scaling in the sequential convex optimization for collision avoidance (Schulman et al., 2014), and further into the multi-stage successive constraint reﬁnement for coverage-oriented placement planning in Malhan et al. (2019) and Kabir et al. (2021). In the latter case, the solution process is divided into two or more stages. In the ﬁrst stage, only a part of the penalties that have fewer but unambiguous attraction regions, such as collision avoidance, are applied. Other penalty terms whose attraction regions have a scattered landscape such as position constraints are enabled in a later stage. For a case of traversing a small ring through a big one, the highest success rate of 63.28% is achieved, if the best penalty sequence among four penalty terms is chosen (Kabir et al., 2021). But for an inexperienced user, the multi-stage tuning is time-consuming with no guaranteed success rate, although a remedy with adaptive search on potentially more costly real experiments is proposed (Kabir et al., 2018).
Manifold learning. Most learning-based methods for trajectory planning with manifold constraints trade timeconsuming ofﬂine training for fast online planning capabilities. Zha et al. (2018) learn the metric of the manifold represented by a graph, so as to select promising sampling direction for online planning. Combined with the projection operation, it results in a high success rate. Qureshi et al. (2021) implement an end-to-end learning from an environment encoding to the next sampling step. However, the above sampling-based manifold learning methods all face restrictions in the free determination of the placement and the starting conﬁguration, which sacriﬁces the whole-path optimality. There exists an exception using an optimizationbased method (Kicki et al., 2023). In this method, manifold constraints are relaxed as penalty terms, whose Lagrange multiplier is learned so as to facilitate fast planning and replanning in dynamic environments such as air hockey. This method does not handle constraint manifolds of path tracking.

Dealing with process-robot speciﬁcations
In manufacturing processes of complex work-piece geometry, the path given by Computer-Aided Manufacturing software can be arbitrarily long with large number of direction changes. After a discretization, with high amount of way-points on the Cartesian path to be tracked, the joint trajectories must also be parameterized with comparable amount of decision variables, owing to the limited approximation capabilities of the trajectory using, for example, B-splines (Lyche et al., 2018b; Takacs and Takacs, 2016). Due to the nonlinear scalability of most parameter optimization methods w.r.t. number of decision variables, it is no more economical to plan the corresponding trajectory as a whole. To allow a linear scalability, Weingartshofer et al. (2023) solve a series of simpliﬁed optimization subproblems sequentially at the cost of the whole-path optimality. An alternative method proposed in Kabir et al. (2021) is to split the path into segments and to optimize for each, subjected to additional inter-segment continuity constraints. These constraints are then converted to a penalty term which serves as the guiding heuristic for a branch-and-bound search. These methods either suffer from computational complexity or sacriﬁce the whole-path optimality by simplifying the computation.
Due to the characteristics of robotic manipulators, the mapping from task space to joint space is not unique. The robots’ capabilities, for example, stiffness, are often a measure in task space but dependent on joint conﬁgurations, which in turn depend on the robots’ placement w.r.t. the work-piece. Several distant conﬁgurations with different placements might exhibit local optimality in desired capabilities. A genetic algorithm is applied in Bachmann et al. (2021) to determine the robot-workpiece placement considering reachability and collision avoidance. The intertwined placement and trajectories are considered in Menasri et al. (2013), Nicola et al. (2018), and Mutti et al. (2021) by means of nested meta-heuristic algorithms, leading to a high number of iterations and a prolonged computation time. The multi-start strategy by sampling random seeds of initial guesses and carrying out the optimization multiple times is utilized in Schulman et al. (2014), De Maeyer et al. (2017), Kabir et al. (2021), and Weingartshofer et al. (2023) to deal with the non-convex nature of the planning problem. However, for complex manufacturing paths, a random generation of initial solutions, even with the help of analytical inverse kinematics, results mostly in non-feasible ones, because not every point of the highly complex path can be reached by all robots.

Conclusion
Existing methods face trade-offs between the computational complexity and the whole-path optimality on the one hand, as well as between an accurate constraint satisfaction and a high convergence speed on the other hand. To the best of our knowledge, there is no method that utilizes both exponential and logarithmic map in the path-constrained trajectory optimization for robots, so as to facilitate a fast convergence with high accuracy of constraint satisfaction. An analogy to these mappings does exist as the horizontal and vertical step in Gabay (1982) and Kupfer (1996), which show the unexplored potential in our preliminary work (Ye et al., 2022). In addition, there exists no method dealing with multiple path segments that leverages parallel computing by means of the promising alternative direction method of multipliers (ADMM) (Boyd et al., 2011).
Preliminaries and system modeling
This section ﬁrst deals with the preliminaries in the kinematic modeling for the trajectory planning of multi-robot manufacturing systems. Afterward, the stiffness of such systems, as one of their crucial capabilities in manufacturing processes, is modeled so that it can be later integrated in the optimization-based planning approach.

Overview of the solution procedure
The algorithm to solve Problem 4 is shown in Algorithm 1. The solution procedure consists of the parallel generation of multiple initial solutions (initGuess), parallel optimization of every single segment of all initiated solutions (optimizeSingleSegment), coordinated intersegment constraint error update (coordinateSegments), and pruning of unfavorable solutions (pruneCandidates). After convergence, one candidate of interconnected trajectory segments is chosen (selectBestCandidate). The residual inter-segment constraint error of this candidate is eliminated in the end (eliminateSegmentResidue) so that it can be executed on the real robots with a B-spline interpolator.

Solution method set
This section illustrates the solution to Problem 4 with the reduced Hessian optimization in core and the parallel computation. An overview of necessary steps is given, before the parallel computation and the ADMM are illustrated. Afterward, in each individual thread of the parallel computation, the reduced Hessian optimization method is used, whose convergence guarantee is provided.

In such a solution procedure, multiple local optima of the trajectory planning problem can be tackled efﬁciently with multiple initial guesses being generated, optimized, compared, and pruned in parallel computation. For a complex multi-segment path, the decentralized optimization in segments has a better scalability w.r.t. path length than solving for the whole trajectories. Moreover, due to the complexity of the path, initial guesses either by random sampling or by manual speciﬁcation of robot-workpiece placement are unlikely to be feasible in terms of whole-path reachability within bounds of joint angles. Infeasible initial guesses are nevertheless accepted, because the joint conﬁgurations at the starting and ﬁnishing path points as well as the placements are all decision variables that can be adjusted in the later optimization process. Simultaneous to the parallel optimization of multiple initial solutions, the segments of Problem 4 are decentrally optimized in a parallel manner in Algorithm 1, so that the scalability w.r.t. path length is enhanced.

to solve (24a, b, c) and consequently (31a, b) have been ob-

necessary secant equations are provided in Byrd and Nocedal

available algorithms, the focus of the current sub-section is on

the convergence of the reduced Hessian method in the robotic

application.

Results
To illustrate the effectiveness of the trajectory planning, the following test cases are experimented with two physically coupled KUKA LBR iiwa 14 R820 robots:
(i) pulling a spring (ii) multi-segment milling
Speciﬁcally, test case (i) veriﬁes the core of the developed reduced Hessian method while utilizing the parallelization with multiple initial solutions. Test case (ii) utilizes the reduced Hessian method and further validates the ADMM intersegment coordinated descend, as well as the stiffness model, so as to demonstrate the usability of the whole method set in reality. All the computations are conducted on a machine with the processor AMD Ryzen 7 PRO 6850U at 2.70 GHz.

Pulling a spring
The test case of pulling a spring imitates manufacturing processes with slowly varying process force such as sheet metal bending. This test case is also described in Ye et al. (2022) where the result with enhanced stiffness and reduced joint load after the optimization is shown. We use the same test case here to examine the robustness of the proposed reduced Hessian method with convergence guarantee in a statistical manner, as well as to show the improvement of efﬁciency compared to Ye et al. (2022).

To reduce the computing time, we improve the convergence behavior using the fast ADMM with the Nesterov acceleration, as given in Algorithm 7 of Goldstein et al. (2014). However, our fast ADMM algorithm differs from Goldstein et al. (2014) in the use of the parallel computation, instead of the sequential one. This means, when calling the function optimizeSingleSegment and solving (23) for the m-th segment, the solution of, for example, the (m À 1)-th segment ezmÀ1 is not the newest result but is from the last iteration. The Nesterov interpolation is only carried out if the augmented Lagrangians (23) of all segments decrease in the last iteration.

Conclusion
Methodologically, the constrained trajectory planning using our reduced Hessian method resolves the conﬂict in existing methods between computational complexity and whole-path optimality, as well as between accurate constraint satisfaction and high convergence speed. Practically, in robot-driven manufacturing processes, the present paper gives a positive answer to the feasibility question on the concept of physically coupling multiple robots for capability enhancements. The concept is made feasible in practice, if the complete method set provided in the present paper is applied.
The proposed method set consists of an efﬁcient reduced Hessian optimization method in its core for the constrained trajectory planning, as well as various supporting techniques to evaluate the collective capabilities, approximate smooth motion trajectories, consider motion limits of robots, deal with highly complex manufacturing paths, and to handle multiple local

optima in the robot conﬁgurations. The core reduced Hessian method is developed with the adaptation to robotic use cases where path and coupling constraints, B-spline trajectory approximation and motion limits are integrated. With these changes, the convergence guarantee in the literature does not apply anymore, but is newly given in the present paper, constituting a theoretical contribution. This advancement in theory is accompanied by an intensive empirical study of the proposed optimization method in comparison with the stateof-the-art, demonstrating the highest success rate of 78.87%, a large reduction of the objective function, low errors in coupling and path tracking, as well as reasonably low correlations of its performance w.r.t. variations in the trajectory parameterization. The practical problem of physical coupling is addressed in the real-world test case of robot-driven milling process. Despite the challenges in the complex path and the multiplicity of solutions in robotic conﬁgurations, the trajectory planning is made possible by the method set with an extension of ADMM parallel computing. Finally, it is shown that the capability of physically coupled robots is enhanced signiﬁcantly with the proposed method set.
